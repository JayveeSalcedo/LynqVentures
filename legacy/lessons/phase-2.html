02 / LEARN

Match evidence to a specific claim.

Validation means gathering evidence about assumptions to inform a decision. An assumption is a claim that could be wrong. A test is a bounded way to investigate it. A signal is information you will interpret with its context and limitations.

Problem

Recent-experience accounts and permitted observation can inform whether a difficulty occurs and matters in a context.

Benefit / clarity

Understanding a benefit statement can indicate clarity. It does not prove adoption or payment.

Behaviour / commitment

Observe a relevant action or specific commitment under clear conditions. Preserve what it does and does not establish.

Payment

Payment assumptions require their own suitable evidence. No money is collected or payment test executed here.

Illustrative example: a visitor says directions sound helpful. That is a reported reaction, not proof they will change behaviour or pay. Ask what claim you are checking before choosing a method.

  1. Review the Discover go/refine/pause decision.
  2. Name one specific assumption for a group/context.
  3. Choose a fitting small check and its limitations.
  4. State supporting and weakening signals without invented thresholds.
  5. Set permission, privacy, owner, timing and review decision.

Negative or ambiguous results can justify refine, repeat or pause. Convenient participants are not automatically representative. A plan or completed lesson is not findings.
