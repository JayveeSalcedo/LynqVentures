02 / LEARN

You can begin before you have an idea.

Entrepreneurship means finding a problem people care about, testing a way to solve it, and building a business that can keep delivering that value. A founder makes decisions under uncertainty. You do not need to know every answer; you need a way to learn.

By the end, you can

  • Explain what a founder actually does.
  • Connect your experience to a problem space.
  • Separate an observation from an assumption.
  • Set a realistic learning commitment.

Three useful words

Venture: a business you are trying to build.
Assumption: something you believe but have not yet tested.
Evidence: something observed or collected that helps you check a belief.

A simple example

An event coordinator notices that visitors struggle to reconnect with people after an event. “People want an app” is an assumption. “Three visitors asked for a way to find someone they met” is an observation. The next step is to understand their experience before choosing a solution.

LYNQ was built in Dubai with a global outlook. Bring your own context: an everyday challenge in a shop, school, workplace, community, or event can be a starting point.

Your task today

Make a honest starting snapshot. “I’m not sure yet” is acceptable for your idea. Be specific about your motivation, skills, available time, and the people you would like to understand. This is a learning baseline, not an investor pitch.

05 / CHALLENGE

Be curious about what could be wrong.

A red-team review looks for weak assumptions so you can improve your plan. It is not a judgment of your potential.

More questions to discuss
  • Are you interested in a customer's problem or only in your solution?
  • Can you reach the people you want to help?
  • Which skill is missing, and how can you learn or find support?
  • What would make you pause or choose a different problem?
